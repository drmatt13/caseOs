# PURPOSE OF REPORT

This report summarizes the postmortem examinations conducted on Nicole Brown Simpson and Ronald Lyle Goldman.

The report documents:

* Cause of death
* Manner of death
* Injury patterns
* Forensic observations
* Estimated time-of-death considerations
* Evidence collected during examination
* Limitations associated with forensic interpretation

This report does not identify a perpetrator and does not determine criminal responsibility.

# CIRCUMSTANCES OF EXAMINATION

The decedent was transported from the Bundy Drive scene to the Office of the Medical Examiner.

The body arrived sealed and accompanied by investigative documentation.

Chain-of-custody documentation was reviewed prior to examination.

---

# EXTERNAL EXAMINATION

## General Condition

Adult female appearing consistent with reported age.

Body received clothed in garments documented separately by evidence personnel.

Signs of extensive blood loss were present.

---

## Injury Overview

Multiple sharp-force injuries were identified.

Injuries involved:

* Neck region
* Upper torso
* Extremities
* Defensive areas

Numerous wounds demonstrated characteristics consistent with sharp-edged instrument trauma.

---

## Neck Injuries

A severe incised wound was present involving the neck region.

Associated findings included:

* Extensive tissue disruption
* Major vascular injury
* Significant hemorrhage

The injury was determined to be a major contributing factor in death.

---

## Defensive-Type Injuries

Several injuries were observed involving:

* Hands
* Forearms
* Upper extremities

These injuries may be consistent with attempts to:

* Deflect blows
* Protect vital areas
* Resist an attack

However, definitive conclusions regarding actions taken during the event cannot be established solely from injury patterns.

---

# INTERNAL EXAMINATION

* Extensive hemorrhage
* Major vascular trauma
* Multiple sharp-force injury tracks

No significant natural disease process was identified that contributed to death.

# TIME OF DEATH ANALYSIS

## Methodology

Time-of-death estimation considered:

* Body temperature
* Livor mortis
* Rigor mortis
* Environmental conditions
* Scene observations
* Witness information
* Medical findings

---

## Findings

Medical examination supports death occurring during the late evening hours of June 12, 1994.

---

## Estimated Range

The available findings support an estimated time-of-death window rather than a precise moment.

Review of investigative materials suggests death likely occurred within a period generally consistent with:

**Approximately 10:15 p.m. to 10:45 p.m.**

---

## Important Limitation

Time-of-death determinations are estimates.

Medical science cannot determine the exact minute of death under the circumstances presented.

Numerous variables may affect postmortem calculations.

---

## Factors Affecting Precision

* Ambient temperature
* Wind conditions
* Body position
* Blood loss
* Clothing
* Individual physiology
* Timing of examination

Accordingly, any estimate should be understood as a range rather than a fixed point.

# ATTACK CHARACTERISTICS

## General Observations

The injury patterns suggest:

* A close-contact assault
* Use of a sharp-edged instrument
* Rapid blood loss
* Significant physical interaction between attacker and victims

---

## Number of Assailants

Medical findings alone do not establish:

---

## Sequence of Injuries

Certain injuries may have occurred before others.

---

Injuries are generally consistent with use of a sharp-edged weapon.

The specific weapon cannot be identified solely from wound examination.

# FORENSIC LIMITATIONS

The medical examination cannot determine:

* Identity of the attacker
* Motive
* Exact sequence of events
* Exact time of death
* Whether evidence was moved after the event
* The specific route traveled by any participant

Such conclusions require integration with investigative evidence.
